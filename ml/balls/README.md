# Ball and hit evidence

Experimental ball lookback is off by default and can be enabled separately on the camera or with the authenticated remote `ballEnabled` setting. The camera keeps a bounded metadata history of moving orange/white candidates and fresh cup masks in a separate worker, looking twelve seconds before each entry. Extra history covers delayed snapshots. A recorded single-cup hit triggers a retrospective search for a short ball trajectory approaching one cup rim. Results are **candidates**, not confirmed hits: an image projection cannot prove that a ball entered a cup. Multiple cups, substantial scene movement, video gaps, stale geometry and competing trajectories cause abstention. Small hand movements can still produce misleading proposals; no hand detector is claimed. Model-accepted moving detections can anchor a short trajectory, allowing up to two frames of current orange pixels near its predicted position when compression changes color or appearance over a red rack. Recovery retains the original model score and selects at most one component per predicted track. Those proposals carry `track-color-recovery` provenance and always require current moving pixels; a prediction alone never creates a ball. White proposals reject orange-motion ghosts and require local brightness contrast. A loaded orange classifier may also inspect compact stationary color components within configured scene bounds, including the first frame. That identity scan classifies at most sixty-four components and retains at most twelve; it supplies no static white-brightness proposals. `static-color-appearance` observations never anchor a trajectory or participate in hit search. Telemetry counts moving and static identity proposals separately. The null registry disables this identity path. The authenticated camera `/vision` report exposes the latest `ballHit`; Sentry receives `ball hit lookback` and `ball lookback stats`. Neither writes a score, a formation, or a training label. Tizen receives no ball inference workload.

Raw video is sampled at 640 pixels wide and approximately 15 fps, without queuing frames. Pixel readback normally runs in an OffscreenCanvas worker; browsers that cannot transfer video bitmaps fall back to a small capture canvas and transfer pixels. Missing workers skip this experimental path. A ready handshake and startup/frame deadlines stop the worker and its timers on failure; state, capture fallback and failure phase are logged. Telemetry snapshots counters before asynchronous logging, so a successful sampler cannot appear idle because its counters were reset. Small, blurred or occluded balls can be missed at that resolution. Sampling pauses in background tabs; lookbacks spanning a pause abstain. Areas are source-video rectangles, with the existing team mapping. Clear or change areas, turn outlines off, or move the input device to reset history.

Manual entry timestamps are delayed and server-clock based; they establish search windows, not the physical hit time. The existing camera snapshot supplies reducer-accepted, still-active entry IDs/times, and the client retains response timing to bound clock uncertainty. Slow snapshots are withheld. A hit entered after a match immediately finishes can disappear from the in-progress snapshot before observation; offline review covers those events. Past recordings do not include historical camera flips/team mappings, so a scored cup ID must not be mapped onto an image without reviewing that session's orientation. Pickups, misses, bounces, held balls and hands are useful hard cases, not automatic positives.

## Local training and review

Keep all footage, patches and per-event labels outside git in a durable private directory; replace the example paths below accordingly. Use the cup pipeline environment plus `pip install -r ml/balls/requirements.txt`. The collector performs read-only SQL over SSH and reuses verified local bucket recordings from the cup pipeline.

```sh
python ml/balls/pipeline.py collect --output /path/private/balls --group SBRIL5OJ5
python ml/balls/pipeline.py windows --dataset /path/private/cups --events /path/private/balls/events.json --output /path/private/balls
python ml/balls/pipeline.py proposals --dataset /path/private/cups --windows /path/private/balls/windows.json --output /path/private/balls --features radial-rgb-local-v2
python ml/balls/pipeline.py review --dataset /path/private/balls --reviewer maintainer
python ml/balls/pipeline.py split --dataset /path/private/balls
python ml/balls/pipeline.py train --dataset /path/private/balls --color orange --id ball-v2 --features radial-rgb-local-v2 --minimum-precision 0.98 --minimum-recall 0.98 --output /path/private/ball-model.json
python ml/balls/pipeline.py evaluate --dataset /path/private/balls --model /path/private/ball-model.json --split valid --output /path/private/ball-valid.json
python ml/balls/pipeline.py evaluate --dataset /path/private/balls --model /path/private/ball-model.json --split test --output /path/private/ball-heldout.json
python ml/balls/pipeline.py export --model /path/private/ball-model.json --report /path/private/ball-heldout.json --tracker-report /path/private/ball-tracker-heldout.json --minimum-precision 0.98 --minimum-recall 0.98 --output apps/web/src/tv/lib/ballModel.json
```

Proposals use moving, compact colored components; the review UI marks the proposed center. Label that object rather than a neighboring ball. Uncertain patches stay out of training. Ball-presence labels do not establish flight, a hit or a particular cup. The patch reviewer may record cup context as playing, removed or unknown; unknown is the default. Review that role against the surrounding video, not cup appearance. Roles remain contextual labels for future membership training, rather than altering segmentation classes. Event windows retain coverage gaps and unchecked `physical_hit_at`/`hit_cup`/`cup_roles` fields for separate video review. Do not convert the recorded move's `cups` into these fields automatically.

The small learned candidate classifier uses source-pixel appearance features and a bounded random forest. The legacy `radial-rgb-color-v1` format has eighteen radial RGB/color features. `train --features radial-rgb-local-v2` selects thirty-seven features, adding direct center color, a small adaptive disk, local contrast and orange-mask shape statistics so tiny balls do not fall between the old grid samples. Local features require exact source samples; the worker selects the declared format/count and disables unsupported registries. New proposals retain features sampled directly from source pixels with the same rounding and bilinear sampling as the worker; JPEG patches are review previews. Older reviewed datasets remain readable through the JPEG fallback, so regenerate proposals when measuring source-pixel parity. It selects depth and confidence from validation only. `--color white` requires reviewed white-ball positives; `--color both` supports a combined dataset. Session-disjoint training/validation/test partitions prevent adjacent frames leaking between splits. Sessions from one camera/night remain related; add different lighting, nights, camera views and negative scenes before claiming general accuracy. Checksum checks reject changed approved images. Training selection and export default to precision ≥0.98 and recall ≥0.98; the explicit minimum flags record the chosen target. Export requires the exact model to pass validation and held-out candidate classification with at least thirty positive and negative examples. It also requires a separate full-tracker report covering two fresh sessions and at least thirty visible-ball positions, including balls that never produced a proposal, with every emitted object reviewed. The full-tracker report binds the model, dataset and runtime source checksums; precision and recall are recomputed from its integer TP/FP/FN counts. Report `scope: "full-visible-ball-tracker"`, `split: "test"`, `modelSha256`, `datasetSha256`, `runtimeSourceSha256` (SHA256 of `ballClassifier.ts`, newline, `ballVision.ts`), `colors`, `sessions`, `metrics`, `includesMissedProposals: true` and `allOutputObjectsReviewed: true`. Candidate classification alone cannot establish ball recognition accuracy. Unsupported colors, uncertain exclusions, held versus unheld cases and reviewed duration must remain explicit in the private audit. Physical-hit localization requires its own ground truth. Never retune after inspecting test results.

The runtime registry `ballModel.json` remains null: private orange-classifier experiments have not passed the current full-tracker promotion requirements, and no verified real white-ball training set is available. Known flight regressions are useful checks but do not establish overall accuracy or confirmed cup entry. Keep weights outside the repository until the exact model and tracker pass the independent gates. Export a passing model into that file and ship through the normal web deploy; inference uses its declared source-pixel feature format and bounded trees in the existing ball worker. Roll back to null to restore motion-only proposals. Unsupported colors remain explicitly untrained proposals.
